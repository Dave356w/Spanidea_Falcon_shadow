# 350 fpm automatic, 2026-08-21 — the Δv gate's out-of-sample test, a blind departure, and a 1.002× arrival

**Dave in the cab, automatic operation at 350 fpm, 14 runs. Build `ce28d9d`**
(`ATmega328PB`, FALCON_LOG 2, §4.2's lateral veto armed), flashed and verified
the same morning. Capture distilled to `falcon_srcs/datasets/260821-101915.log`.

**Every run behaved.** 14 latched, 14 released correctly, no failsafe, no
release on a moving car, no sensor faults beyond 2 isolated `ACC-STAT read
FAILED` (against 12 in 33 runs on 08-20 and 21 in 82 on 08-18 — the rate is
lower here, not new).

Three results, in order of how much they should change what happens next.

# 2. 🔴 Two runs went BLIND — no departure burst at all

Runs 3 and 11 latched `(polled)` and emitted **no `BURST k=dep` and no `JOGV`**.
Checked line by line in the raw capture: the *arrival* burst printed normally on
both, so this is not a dropped line. The departure burst was never written.

Only the any-motion branch emits it (S10/B3), so **any-motion did not fire on
those two departures.** Consequence: every candidate rule that reads the
departure burst — §4.3's Δv gate, the jog verdict, anything built on shape — is
**blind on 2 of 14 runs at contract speed.** That moves B3 out of
"instrumentation backlog" and into "prerequisite".

**2 of 14 overall, and both on 5-4 down**, which ran three times. Run 13, same
floor pair, same direction, produced both burst and verdict. So it is strongly
localised and **not deterministic**.

## 2.1 What is ruled out

- ⛔ **Not the re-arm blank.** Zero `any-motion ignored, re-arm blanking` lines
  in the whole session, and both blind runs latched ~2 minutes after entering
  MONITORING. This is *not* the 2026-08-18 mechanism, which is the first thing
  anyone will assume.
- ⛔ **Not a gentler departure.** Peak `|a − zero|` in the 2 s after the latch:
  **0.634** and **0.631** on the blind runs, against **0.600–0.640** on the
  twelve that produced bursts. The excursion is the same size.

⚠️ **What that second point does NOT establish.** The log is 25 Hz and
decimated; any-motion triggers on a per-sample delta at **100 Hz** sustained
over `ANYMOTION_DURATION` 5 samples. A large total excursion built from small
per-sample steps is exactly what this log cannot distinguish from a steep one.
So "the transient was the same size" is measured; "the sensor should have fired"
is not closed by this data. **The 2026-08-07 datasheet hazard — any-motion
comparing against a latched reference, with nothing checking — remains the
candidate that fits, and it remains uninstrumented.**

# 4. ⭐ The Δv gate passed its out-of-sample test

`graph/dv_replay.py` (added with this note) scores §4.3's proposal: integrate
the departure burst, and use Δv to tell automatic operation from inspection —
the mode question `falcon_ramp_priority_2026-08-20` §5 leaves open, and the only
thing that would make a ramp-veto shippable.

**This session was the falsifier.** The rule was derived from a corpus whose
350 fpm population was 5 bursts from 2026-08-11 on firmware three weeks old.
These 14 runs are a different day, a different build and a machine the rule
never saw.

- **12 of 12 runs with a burst scored AUTOMATIC.** Range 0.932–1.318.
- The two weakest, 0.932 and 0.965, are now **the weakest automatic runs on
  file** — they replaced the previous floor of 0.984. Adding them to the
  labelled corpus *lowered* the headline, and it still separates.

Corpus after this session, `run`-labelled, median-of-8 zero, 2.4 s window:

| population | n | min | median | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| automatic | **55** | **0.932** | 1.208 | 1.391 |
| inspection | 26 | 0.000 | 0.053 | **0.452** |

**Separated 2.1×.** Across *every* burst in the corpus, labelled or not, the
inspection ceiling is **0.517** and no threshold in 0.60–0.90 produces a single
false-automatic in 141 inspection bursts.

## 4.1 And the veto behind it

Borrowing `ramp_priority_replay`'s parser and verdicts unchanged, adding only
the gate (`python graph/dv_replay.py --veto ../logs`):

| rule | MOVING | OK | NONE | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `peak` | 2 | 206 | 0 | what ships today |
| `ramp_veto` | 1 | 111 | **96** | safe and unusable |
| **`dv_veto`** | 1 | 202 | **5** | the candidate |

**It prevents the confirmed 2026-08-20 release on a moving car** (`260820-142248:6504`,
lateral 0.311, Δv 1.163 — gate applies, release refused). Every inspection
capture goes from 56 / 16 / 6 / 5 / 2 NO-RELEASE under the plain veto to
**zero**. The 5 remaining NO-RELEASE are all on automatic captures.

⚠️ **It is not strictly safer than what ships.** `260812-112254:14418` is `OK`
under the peak and `MOVING` under both vetoes — the veto releases on the ramp,
which lands later, and on that run the tail at ramp time reads as motion. Its
lateral is 0.052, i.e. inside the band the instrument itself calls unresolved,
so this is a weak call against a strong one (0.311) prevented. It should be
resolved before anything ships.

## 4.2 Two method points that decide the result

- **The zero estimate is load-bearing.** Any-motion fires part-way up the ramp,
  so the later pre-trigger samples already carry signal: a mean over all 20 puts
  the "zero" at 69 mm/s² — mostly signal — and collapses the separation from
  2.1× to **1.03×**. A **median of the first 8** is what the tool uses, and
  anything from 2 to 12 gives the same conclusion.
- **A median, not a mean.** `260818-105317` run 17 opens
  `[40, 60, 133, -697, -206, 3077]`; a mean puts the zero at 401 mm/s² and
  manufactures 1.03 m/s of Δv from a burst whose post-trigger samples never
  exceed 460. **That single artefact was the worst false-automatic in the
  corpus** and it is a zero-fit failure, not motion.
- ⛔ **The mode labels do not come from ramp availability.** Using "has a ramp
  verdict" as ground truth for "is automatic" would be circular, since ramp
  availability is what Δv exists to replace. Every label in the tool's `MODE`
  table names the session-note sentence that establishes it, and captures no
  note describes are `unknown` and scored separately.

# 5. The bottom terminal is a different regime

Eight of these runs were single-floor. **Six behaved exactly like the four-floor
express runs** (Δv 1.27–1.32, ramp 595–611). The two involving **floor 1** did
not:

```
  2-1 down   dv 0.932   ramp 523   mx/xs 1.2x   arm q=5
  1-2 up     dv 0.965   ramp 509   mx/xs 1.1x   arm q=5
```

Three independent quantities move together, which is what makes it an effect
rather than noise. **It is the terminal, not the run length** — the earlier
reading of this session as "short hops are weaker" was wrong and is retracted.

This is the first measurement of S4's terminal case at contract speed. It did
not produce S4's failure — both runs armed and released — but it arrived at the
minimum with nothing in reserve.

# 7. What this changes

1. **B3 is now a prerequisite, not instrumentation.** 2 of 14 runs give the
   departure burst nothing to work with. It needs ~180 bytes against 144 free;
   `-mcall-prologues` is worth 228 and is the obvious source.
2. **The Δv gate is worth building unarmed**, the way the ramp detector was:
   print `DV n= dv= auto=` at the burst freeze and fly it before it gates
   anything. It costs one integral over samples already in RAM.
3. **The any-motion blind spot needs its own instrument.** The log cannot
   distinguish "engine did not fire" from "ramp too gentle at 100 Hz", and that
   is the same blindness the 2026-08-07 datasheet review warned about.
4. ⛔ **Nothing here clears the installation of 2026-08-20**, and nothing here
   is a shipped fix. The standing instruction stands.
