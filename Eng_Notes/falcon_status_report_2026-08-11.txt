FALCON MOVEMENT-ALARM FIRMWARE — TEST STATUS REPORT
Date: 2026-08-11
Testing: full-day hoistway session (cab interior + cartop), Dave W.

SUMMARY
A major usability defect was fixed and verified today, the device's
behavior at full speed was characterized for the first time, and the
detection limits of the current design are now measured rather than
estimated. One serious hardware issue remains open and is the top
priority.

COMPLETED TODAY

1. Jog false-alarm defect: FIXED AND VERIFIED.
   Previously, any short inspection movement ("jog") locked the alarm on
   for 4 minutes, making the device impractical during normal inspection
   work. A new classifier now recognizes a jog by its motion signature
   and releases the alarm after a ~4 second chirp. Verified live on the
   cartop: 5 out of 5 jogs released correctly, 20 of 20 classifier
   decisions correct across the day. The safety-critical failure
   direction (silencing the alarm on a genuinely moving car) has
   measured margin of 2x, and all known drift directions fail safe.

2. Normal-speed (350 fpm) automatic operation: NOW CHARACTERIZED.
   Four full-speed express runs from inside the cab. All stops released
   the alarm correctly — but with as little as 1% margin. The release
   currently works on luck, not headroom. The drive's braking profile
   was measured precisely (constant 0.6 m/s2 both directions), which
   fully defines the robust fix: a "ramp detector" keyed to sustained
   deceleration. Design is justified by data; implementation is the next
   firmware work item.

3. Inspection-mode stop detection: floor measured.
   Gentle "leveling" stops at terminal landings produce the weakest
   detectable arrival signal: 0.573 vs an alarm-release threshold of
   0.45 (27% margin, highly repeatable). Adequate, but the margin
   depends on each installation's controller behavior — recommend a
   brief per-site verification step in any deployment procedure.

OPEN ITEMS (priority order)

1. POWER: the device browns out after ~4 minutes of continuous alarm on
   fresh batteries (rail sag under sounder load, not battery depletion —
   the low-battery warning never triggers). This caps every long-alarm
   scenario and is the product's most serious open issue. A firmware
   mitigation (halved sounder duty cycle) is in place but unverified;
   bench measurement with a meter is scheduled next.
2. Ramp detector for normal-speed stops (design complete, not built).
3. Intermittent device lockup: three occurrences today requiring an
   external reset; cost one test run. Root cause not yet identified.
4. Battery telemetry produced no valid readings for one multi-hour
   period (recovered after clean restart); under investigation.

OVERALL
The device is now solid in its primary use case (inspection operation),
usable during inspection jogging for the first time, and its remaining
detection gap at normal speed has a data-backed fix designed. The power
issue is the main risk to the product concept and gates further
long-duration testing.
